## Generic template, preserved verbatim

You are an elite digital art director, interaction designer, conversion strategist, and frontend engineer. Create a production-quality, scroll-crafted, layered website for a luxury product.

Do not begin by coding. First convert the inputs below into a precise creative direction, narrative, layer system, and scroll choreography. Then implement and visually test the result.

## Product inputs

- Product: [PRODUCT]
- Product category: [CATEGORY]
- Brand name: [BRAND]
- Price position: [PREMIUM / LUXURY / ULTRA-LUXURY]
- Primary buyer: [PERSON]
- Buyer’s underlying tension, unmet desire, or problem: [PAIN OR DESIRE]
- Transformation or promise: [PROMISE]
- Primary action: [PURCHASE / RESERVE / BOOK APPOINTMENT / JOIN WAITLIST]
- Important product truths: [MATERIALS, CRAFT, PERFORMANCE, PROVENANCE]
- Variants or collection: [VARIANTS]
- Brand character: [THREE TO FIVE ADJECTIVES]
- The brand must never feel: [UNDESIRED TRAITS]
- Available assets: [PRODUCT PHOTOS, CUTOUTS, VIDEO, LOGO, TYPE, COLOURS]
- Inspiration sites or components: [URLS OR SCREENSHOTS]
- Technical environment: [EXISTING STACK OR “CHOOSE AN APPROPRIATE STACK”]

If the product assets are insufficient for a convincing layered composition, identify exactly what is missing before implementation. Do not hide weak assets behind gradients, visual effects, or generic stock photography.

## Core objective

The website should feel like a directed luxury experience, not a collection of sections or UI cards. It must tell one coherent story as the visitor scrolls.

Use scrolling to reveal meaning, product construction, material quality, provenance, transformation, or desire. Motion must support the product’s promise. It must never exist merely to demonstrate animation skill.

The commercial message must remain clear. Establish:

1. The person: exactly whom this product is for.
2. The tension: what this person lacks, rejects, desires, or wants to become.
3. The promise: what owning or experiencing this product changes.
4. The proof: materials, craft, engineering, provenance, scarcity, service, or demonstrated performance.
5. The action: what the visitor should do next.

For a luxury product, “pain” may be an emotional or aesthetic tension rather than a practical problem.

## Define the creative concept

Before implementation, present:

1. A one-sentence creative concept.
2. The emotional journey from first impression to final action.
3. The visual metaphor governing the site.
4. A restrained colour system.
5. A typography system using no more than two principal type families.
6. The signature interaction that visitors should remember.
7. A short explanation of why the concept fits this particular product and buyer.

Do not copy another brand’s trade dress, composition, wording, or signature interaction. Use references to infer quality, pacing, hierarchy, and interaction principles.

## Construct an explicit layer system

Treat important scenes as depth compositions, not flat backgrounds.

For each immersive scene, define:

- Background plane: environment, architecture, landscape, light, or texture.
- Mid-ground plane: contextual objects, shadows, particles, or secondary imagery.
- Typography plane: headline and essential copy.
- Product plane: an isolated, high-resolution product or material detail.
- Foreground plane: a close texture, surface, frame, shadow, fabric, rock, mist, or architectural element.
- Interface plane: navigation, progress indication, controls, and calls to action.

Specify the z-order and intended occlusions. At least one hero element should visibly pass in front of another plane, such as a product crossing the headline or a foreground material partially covering the product. The overlap must feel intentional, and essential copy must remain readable.

Do not simulate depth by stacking rectangular cards.

## Scroll choreography

Create one primary scroll narrative rather than many unrelated effects.

Use a pinned or sticky scene only where it adds meaning. A strong pattern is:

- The atmosphere establishes the world.
- The headline enters or is uncovered.
- The product separates from its surroundings.
- Foreground and background move at different rates.
- A material or mechanical detail is revealed.
- The scene resolves into normal document flow and explanatory content.

Move the planes at restrained, different rates. The background should normally move least; the focal product and foreground may move more. Small changes in scale, position, masking, blur, light, or rotation are preferable to dramatic continuous motion.

Possible product-appropriate interactions include:

- A product emerging through a circular or architectural mask.
- An exterior view becoming an interior or close material view.
- A watch, mechanism, or device moving from complete form to an exploded detail.
- A bottle, garment, bag, or object changing finish or colour as variants are introduced.
- A macro material texture resolving into the complete product.
- A horizontal collection sequence controlled by vertical scrolling.
- Oversized typography moving behind and in front of the product.

Select only one or two of these. Do not use all of them.

Animations must be reversible when the user scrolls backward. Avoid long entrance animations that make content inaccessible. Do not trap the visitor in excessive pinned scrolling.

## Page narrative

Adapt the architecture to the product rather than mechanically following a template. A suitable luxury-product sequence might contain:

1. A cinematic layered hero with one clear promise and one action.
2. A quiet editorial section explaining the product philosophy.
3. A scroll-driven material, mechanism, or craftsmanship reveal.
4. A provenance or making section using large imagery and restrained copy.
5. A collection or variant sequence.
6. Concrete reasons to believe: specifications, makers, origin, warranty, service, or scarcity.
7. An ownership moment showing the product in its intended world.
8. A decisive final action.

Alternate immersive scenes with quiet sections. The quiet sections create contrast and prevent the site from becoming exhausting.

## Visual language

Aim for editorial luxury rather than generic “luxury styling.”

Use:

- Exceptional, product-specific imagery.
- Large type with disciplined line breaks.
- A controlled serif/sans-serif contrast where appropriate.
- Generous negative space.
- Thin rules, small indexes, restrained captions, and precise alignment.
- A narrow palette with one meaningful accent.
- Tactile material detail.
- Asymmetry that remains balanced.
- Sections with distinct compositions but a shared visual grammar.

Avoid:

- Glassmorphism.
- Purple or blue gradient backgrounds without a brand reason.
- Glowing blobs.
- Repeated rounded cards.
- Generic pill buttons everywhere.
- Excessive drop shadows.
- Stock “luxury lifestyle” clichés.
- Meaningless particles.
- Animation on every heading.
- A conventional SaaS landing-page structure.
- Filling every empty area.
- Making every product category look like a gaming or technology website.

## Conversion discipline

The hero must communicate the product category, emotional promise, and next action without requiring the visitor to understand the animation.

Make the principal action visible early and repeat it only at natural decision points. Motion may build desire, but specifications, price context, availability, service, and trust must remain easy to find.

The level of motion must fit the buyer. Do not give a conservative jewellery client, mature medical buyer, or heritage customer the same interaction language as a youth-oriented headphone or fashion launch.

## Implementation

Use the existing project’s stack and conventions. If no stack exists, choose a lightweight, maintainable approach suitable for the required motion.

Prefer semantic HTML, modern CSS, optimized images, and a focused scroll-animation system. Use WebGL only when it materially improves the product story and the available assets justify it. A well-built 2.5D composition using transparent product cutouts is preferable to gratuitous 3D.

Requirements:

- Responsive at desktop, tablet, and mobile sizes.
- No horizontal overflow.
- Stable layout without visible shifting.
- Smooth scrolling without hijacking native controls.
- Keyboard-accessible navigation and controls.
- Sufficient contrast and readable text over imagery.
- A meaningful `prefers-reduced-motion` version.
- Mobile compositions redesigned rather than merely scaled down.
- Product imagery remains sharp on high-density displays.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold assets.
- Preload only the assets required for the first scene.
- Do not sacrifice usability or load time for animation.

On mobile, reduce the number of active depth planes, shorten pinned sequences, and preserve the product-message-action hierarchy.

## Visual verification and iteration

After implementation:

1. Capture the hero, each major scroll state, and the final action at desktop and mobile widths.
2. Scroll through the complete page in both directions.
3. Check every layer transition for clipping, accidental overlap, unreadable copy, and sudden jumps.
4. Confirm that each animation strengthens a specific idea.
5. Remove any effect that merely decorates the page.
6. Check that the result still communicates when reduced motion is enabled.
7. Compare the result against the stated buyer, promise, brand character, and inspiration—not merely against general ideas of beauty.
8. Perform at least one refinement pass after visual inspection.

## Definition of done

The result is complete only when:

- The page has one clear creative thesis.
- The product is the visual protagonist.
- The principal scene contains real, perceptible depth planes.
- Scrolling changes the composition or understanding, not merely element opacity.
- The page contains quiet editorial intervals.
- Typography and spacing feel intentionally art-directed.
- The design is recognizably appropriate for this product category.
- The commercial promise and action remain clear.
- Desktop and mobile versions both work.
- The reduced-motion version remains coherent.
- No generic AI design clichés remain.
